The bottom line

What worked was new information, not new algorithms. More real training data (re-cut augmentation, +0.009 for the trees), the volatility state at the end of the history (+0.007), and the surprise stream of a learned normal-behaviour model (+0.005) were the big steps. A Bayesian change-point posterior with a prior learned from real breaks gave the most consistent gain (+0.002, every fold).

What did not work, mostly, was re-measuring the same thing. About fifteen new statistic families (distribution shape, kernel tests, ordinal patterns, entropy, long memory, embeddings, a window classifier) turned out to re-read variance and tails, which the trees already had. New ways of combining (stacking, ranking losses, bigger networks) lost or tied. Training data that did not match the real breaks (synthetic breaks, the 2025 edition) hurt.

Why the score is hard to move: most remaining misses are breaks that leave the variance unchanged, where every family tried scores 0.50–0.52. On top of that, the variance of unbroken series wanders on its own about as much as a typical break changes it.

Where it stands: the 0.6483 blend is built, verified and passes crunch test, but has not been submitted, so the CV-to-leaderboard offset is unknown. Two leads are open: multi-horizon heads for the normal-behaviour model (+0.0047 at the gate; built, awaiting its paired run) and frozen variance forecasts (a weak pass).

Ground rules: the task, the metric, the test

The task and the metric (TS-AUC)

Framework

Each series has a break-free history (1,000–5,000 points, z-scored) and then an online stretch of 10–1,000 points, read one point at a time. Half the series break somewhere in the online stretch. After every new point we output a score for "the break has already happened". At each online step the metric asks: across all series still running, do the broken ones score higher than the unbroken ones? It averages those per-step AUCs, giving most weight to steps where both kinds are common (steps 100–700 carry about 84% of the weight).

At step \(t\), let \(P_t\) be the series with \(\tau \le t\) (positives) and \(N_t\) those without a break by \(t\), with scores \(s_{i,t}\):

$$\mathrm{AUC}_t=\frac{1}{|P_t||N_t|}\sum_{i\in P_t}\sum_{j\in N_t}\Big(\mathbb 1[s_{i,t}>s_{j,t}]+\tfrac12\mathbb 1[s_{i,t}=s_{j,t}]\Big),\qquad \text{TS-AUC}=\frac{\sum_t |P_t||N_t|\,\mathrm{AUC}_t}{\sum_t |P_t||N_t|}.$$

Training rows are weighted to match: at step \(t\) a positive row gets \(|N_t|/2\) and a negative \(|P_t|/2\), so each class's total weight at that step is proportional to the metric's weight.

Decision rule used for every change, written before each run: per-fold paired difference \(d_f\) against the current best, with \(\bar d \ge +0.003\), \(\bar d > 2\,\mathrm{SE}\) where \(\mathrm{SE}=\mathrm{sd}(d)/\sqrt5\), and at least 4 of 5 folds positive. For the GRU it must hold on both seeds. Late in the project, blend members that cost nothing extra at inference were also accepted at about +0.001 when consistent (5/5). Fold noise is about ±0.006–0.008 per run, so unpaired comparisons mean little.

Terms used below

OOFout-of-fold: each series scored by a model that never saw it in training.
Gate / oraclea cheap screening script run before a full CV run. An oracle knows the break position (the most favourable setting).
Single-feature TS-AUCTS-AUC of one feature used directly as the score. 0.5 = no signal.
Variance-unchanged slicebreak series whose variance barely moved, plus no-break controls: the slice where the models are weakest.
Re-cuta new training series made by moving a real series' history/online boundary (real values, exact labels).
BOCPD, NBMBayesian online change-point posterior; normal-behaviour model (a GRU pretrained without labels to forecast the next residual).
Membersthe three models in the blend: XGBoost trees, a GRU (two seeds), a kernel SVM.
M / Hin the "why" column: M = the reason was measured directly; H = a hypothesis that fits the data but was not tested on its own.

Why things worked or failed: the cross-cutting lessons

1. New information beat new algorithms. The large steps added something the models did not know: more real series, the end-of-history volatility level, a learned forecaster's surprise. New model types and objectives added +0.001 at most, and several lost.

Re-cuts +0.0093, volatility state +0.0070, NBM +0.0049 (trees). SVM +0.0012 (blend); stacking −0.0013 to −0.0044; ranking −0.017 / −0.0011; bigger GRUs ≈ 0.

2. Most new statistic families re-measured variance and tails. A new statistic can look different (low pairwise correlation) and still carry information the trees already combine from several features. Strength as a single feature did not predict a gain.

MMD ρ 0.81–0.91 with Anderson–Darling; matrix-profile novelty ρ 0.80–0.85 with the variance ratio; long-range VR(4) ρ 0.77–0.80 with lag-1 AC; richer BOCPD ρ 0.97 with plain BOCPD. The distribution tests had ρ 0.31–0.60 with any single feature, yet got 1.4–1.8% of gain. The pair classifier's pc_smax ranked 3rd by gain with a net change of −0.0001.

3. Most remaining misses look undetectable at this data size. Where the variance did not change, every family tried scores close to chance. The bigger problem is the unbroken series: their variance wanders as much as a typical break changes it.

Variance-unchanged slice 0.50–0.52 for every family; breaks unchanged on all 5 simple axes (4.4%) score 0.487; positives 0–10 points after the break 0.528. No-break log-variance sd 0.41 over 100 points.

4. Frozen references keep the evidence; adaptive ones erase it. Anything that updates its idea of "normal" with online data absorbs the break it should detect.

Adaptive error scale worse than fixed; frozen GARCH path +0.007; longer NBM training loses steps 700+ (H: adapts like GARCH); per-series NBM fine-tuning worsens held-out NLL; running max and EWMA of the score hurt.

5. The history's equation is already known, and the data is time-reversible. Better forecasting of the history is not where the signal is.

Nonlinear mean: MSE +0.78% (worse); extended GARCH +0.0002 nats; NBM vs GARCH-t on the history +0.0001 nats; backward and forward nets reach the same NLL.

6. The models are short of data, but only data from the same distribution helps.

Learning curve +0.015 per doubling; real re-cuts +0.009. More of the same values (×4, trimming) ≈ 0; synthetic breaks −0.0022 (wrong time-shape); 2025 data −0.0057 (different population, wrong prior).

7. The blend absorbs a member's gain, unless that member gains a new kind of information. Giving the GRU the trees' strongest new feature lifts the GRU, not the blend.

BOCPD GRU +0.0038 / +0.0067 alone → +0.0004 in the blend; NBM GRU +0.0056 / +0.0042 → +0.0007. Counter-examples: NBM trees kept about two thirds of their gain in the blend; the SVM gained +0.0049 from NBM inputs, which it could not see before.

8. Single-feature gains shrink several-fold inside the model: about 4–6× into the trees and 6–11× into the blend. A screen can kill an idea, but it cannot size one.

BOCPD: +0.011 single → +0.0019 trees → +0.0010 blend. NBM: +0.019 single → +0.0049 trees → +0.0032 blend.

9. Cheap gates failed in three different ways, so the paired CV run is the only test.

False positive: embedding drift +0.004 in a weak gate base → +0.0001 in the trees. Blind: the Chronos-2 gate's positive control came out at −0.0044 for information worth +0.0049 in production. Go-then-fail: the pair-classifier gate said GO, then the trees showed −0.0001. A linear "blend + λ·x" gate saw about a tenth of a real gain (+0.0003 vs +0.0032).

10. Noise discipline mattered. Rules written before each run, two or three seeds for neural nets, and full-size runs kept noise from being adopted.

Single GRU runs vary ±0.01; the NBM sweep's round-1 leaders fell by 0.006 on a second seed; the entropy hypothesis "passed" at 600 series and vanished at 10,000; equation F3g showed +0.05 at 300 series and +0.007 at 1,000.

1The base: streaming features and a tree model

1.1Streaming feature kernel: AR whitening, running means, windows and scans

Adopted74 features 0.594 → 270 features 0.6155; 388 features −0.005; without context −0.004
Intuition

Breaks here are subtle: almost never a mean shift, mostly a change in variance, roughness, tails or dependence. So first remove what is predictable (an AR(10) model fitted on the history), then watch 32 simple "increment series" built from each new point and its residual: squares, absolute values, tail flags, lag products, ranks, differences at several lags. For each one, ask whether its recent average has drifted from what the history says is normal, using all online points, the last 10/30/100 points, and a scan over every possible start time. The scan matters because we don't know when the break happened; the best start time adapts to it.

Math

AR residual \(e_t = z_t-\sum_{k=1}^{10}\hat\phi_k z_{t-k}\), \(\hat\phi\) by least squares on the history. For each increment \(g_k(t)\) the history gives a centre \(m_k\) and a long-run variance by batch means, \(\hat\sigma^2_{\mathrm{LR},k}=b\cdot\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\text{block means})\), so autocorrelated series are not over-flagged. With \(S_k(j,t)=\sum_{s=j}^{t}(g_k(s)-m_k)\):

$$Z_k^{W}(t)=\frac{S_k(t-W+1,t)}{\sqrt{W\hat\sigma^2_{\mathrm{LR},k}}},\qquad \mathrm{Scan}_k(t)=\max_{j\le t-L_{\min}+1}\frac{S_k(j,t)}{\sqrt{(t-j+1)\,\hat\sigma^2_{\mathrm{LR},k}}}\ \ (\text{and the min}),$$

from cumulative sums in \(O(t)\) per step, plus a decile chi-square, EWMAs and 5 history descriptors. The same compiled kernel runs in training and inference, so features are identical and causal.

Why it worked: it measures exactly the kinds of change these breaks make, and the scan adapts to the unknown break time. What failed around it: 388 features (finer tails, extra windows) lost −0.005 (redundant features split importance); dropping the history context lost −0.004 (context tells the trees how to read each type of series); 32 more history descriptors added nothing.

1.2Per-step tree classifier (LightGBM, later XGBoost on GPU)

Adopted0.6155; XGBoost same score, 2× faster; logistic regression 0.5939
Intuition

Turn the stream into a table: one row per (series, every 3rd online step), label 1 once the break has happened. Gradient-boosted trees learn which combinations of features mean "break", for example "tail scan high and this kind of series". Heavy regularisation (at least 2,000 rows per leaf) keeps them from memorising individual series, each of which contributes about 170 near-identical rows.

Math
$$\min_F\ \sum_{i,t} w_{i,t}\,\ell\big(y_{i,t},F(\mathbf x_{i,t})\big),\qquad \ell(y,F)=-y\log\sigma(F)-(1-y)\log(1-\sigma(F)),$$

with the metric-matched weights \(w_{i,t}\). 31 leaves, learning rate 0.03, min 2,000 rows per leaf, feature fraction 0.5; rounds picked on the cross-validated average curve.

Why it worked: the weights align training with the metric, and trees combine many weak statistics conditioned on the type of series. Logistic regression got within 0.022 and blended for only +0.002, below the bar.

1.3"Predict the next point" bake-off: better models of normal

Exhaustedraw 0.5565 → AR(1) 0.575 → AR(10) 0.590; everything else flat (3,000 series, stacked LightGBM)
Intuition

If you can predict the next point better, surprises stand out more. We tried AR of order 1 to 50, order by BIC, ridge AR, random walk, EWMA, an adaptive error scale, Student-t surprise, and a nonlinear GBM correction on top of AR(10). AR(10) captured all the linearly predictable structure; the nonlinear correction explained −0.05% of the remaining error.

Math

Surprise \(e_t^2/\hat\sigma^2\) or \(-\log f_\nu(e_t/\hat\sigma)\). The adaptive scale \(\hat\sigma_t^2=\lambda\hat\sigma^2_{t-1}+(1-\lambda)e_t^2\) tracks a variance break, so \(e_t/\hat\sigma_t\) returns to normal after it: the evidence is erased.

Why it failed: there is no more linear structure to find, and adaptive scales absorb the break. The later normal-behaviour model (6.1) helped for a different reason: not a better forecast, but a stream that behaves usefully after a break.

2A second model and how to combine

2.1GRU sequence model fed the top-20 kernel features

Adopted0.6152 alone; 50/50 blend with the trees 0.6244 (+0.0086)
Intuition

A recurrent network reads the series point by point and keeps a memory, so it can learn its own way of accumulating evidence. On raw inputs it was weaker than the trees (about 0.60). Giving it the 20 most important kernel features lifted it to the trees' level, and because it makes different mistakes, averaging the two gave +0.0086. The last 100 history points warm up its memory.

Math
$$\mathbf h_t=\mathrm{GRU}\big(\mathbf h_{t-1},\ \mathrm{GELU}(A\mathbf x_t)\big),\qquad p_t=\sigma\big(\mathrm{MLP}(\mathbf h_t)\big).$$

Inputs: \(z_t\), \(e_t\), \(e_t^2-1\), \(|e_t|-0.8\), \(e_te_{t-1}\), \(\mathbb 1[|e_t|>3]\), 6 history descriptors, an online flag, \(\log(1+t)\), the top-20 features as \(\mathrm{clip}(\mathrm{std}(\mathrm{sign}(x)\log(1+|x|)),\pm6)\). Same weighted cross-entropy as the trees. Hidden 64, AdamW, cosine schedule, early stopping.

Why it worked: different errors from the trees. 40 input features instead of 20 was worse (0.6096 vs 0.6152).

2.2Blending: a fixed weighted average of logits

Adoptedplain logit average = per-step standardised (0.6244); cross-fitted weights 0.6429 vs 0.6427
Intuition

The first blend standardised scores across all series at each step, which is impossible in production because series are scored one at a time. A plain weighted average of each model's logit, with one global scale per model, scored the same, so that is what ships.

Math
$$s_{i,t}=\sum_m w_m\,\frac{\mathrm{logit}\,p^{(m)}_{i,t}}{c_m},\qquad \sum_m w_m=1,\qquad w=(0.5,\,0.3,\,0.2)\ \text{for trees, GRU pair, SVM}.$$
Why it works: the weight plateau is flat and weights chosen on four folds score the same on the fifth, so the blend numbers are honest.

2.3Smoothing the score over time (EWMA, running max)

RejectedEWMA half-life 1–8 rows −0.0004 to −0.0078; decaying max 0 to −0.0014; plain running max 0.589

Once a break has happened it stays happened, so maybe the score should never go down, or be smoothed: \(\tilde s_t=\alpha s_t+(1-\alpha)\tilde s_{t-1}\) or \(\tilde s_t=\max(s_t,\delta\tilde s_{t-1})\).

Why it failed: the models already accumulate evidence; smoothing only adds lag, and a running max locks in early false alarms.

2.4Stacking, and separate weights per step range

Rejectedstacking −0.0013 to −0.0044 (0–1/5); per-step-range weights −0.0000 ± 0.0002

Instead of fixed weights, let a monotone LightGBM combine the members, \(s=G(s^{(1)},s^{(2)},s^{(3)},t,\mathbf c_i)\), cross-fitted by fold; or let the weights differ by step range.

Why it failed: with history descriptors as inputs the stacker memorised groups of series (each has about 170 near-identical rows); its curve fell from the first checkpoint. Without them it still lost. One weighted sum is already the right combiner. (Another team's +0.008 from a ranking stack did not reproduce here.)

2.5Ranking objectives instead of classification

Rejectedtrees rank:pairwise −0.017 (fold 0); GRU pair loss −0.0011 ± 0.0014 (2/5)
$$\mathcal L=\sum_t\ \sum_{i\in P_t}\sum_{j\in N_t}\log\big(1+e^{-(s_{i,t}-s_{j,t})}\big)\quad(\text{GRU: } 0.3\,\mathcal L_{\text{rank}}+0.7\,\mathcal L_{\text{BCE}}).$$
Why it failed H: the metric only ranks series at the same step, so this looks ideal, but the pairwise signal is noisier than plain per-row labels; the trees declined after 100 rounds.

2.6Bigger or gated GRUs, more seeds, other inputs

Rejected8 capacity settings × 2 seeds within noise; top-20 from all features −0.0008; 4 seeds +0.0002 in the blend

Two or three layers, hidden 96–128, learned channel gates \(\tilde{\mathbf x}_t=\mathbf g\odot\mathbf x_t\) (fixed, per series, per step), 40 features, top-20 picked from all tree features, seeds 2 and 3. The learned gates showed what the GRU values: time, tail scans and dependence features, not raw values. Auxiliary targets (time since break, break type) were built but only smoke-tested. An earlier single-fold sweep of ramped labels, memory bias, positive boost, dropout, learning rate and hidden size was indistinguishable from seed noise (about ±0.01).

Why it failed: the GRU is short of data, not capacity. Seed averaging helps the GRU alone (+0.0024) but the blend already averages that noise away.

3More training data

3.1Boundary re-cut augmentation

Adoptedtrees +0.0093 ± 0.0021 (5/5); GRU +0.0058 / +0.0088; ×4 vs ×2 +0.0007 ± 0.0014
Intuition

The learning curve said doubling the data is worth about +0.015 (25% / 50% / 100% of the series: 0.595 / 0.613 / 0.627 on fold 0). New, correctly labelled series can be made from real ones by moving the history/online boundary. Move it earlier and the end of the history becomes online data (still break-free, so the break just happens later). Move it later and pre-break online points join the history.

Math

Shift \(s>0\) (earlier): \(h'=h_{1:n-s},\ o'=(h_{n-s+1:n},o_{1:m}),\ \tau'=\tau+s\). Shift \(s<0\) (later), only if no break in the first \(|s|\) online points: \(h'=(h,o_{1:|s|}),\ o'=o_{|s|+1:m},\ \tau'=\tau-|s|\). Shifts drawn so online length and break position keep their training distributions; variants train only in folds where their parent trains.

Why it worked: real values, exact labels, a data-limited model. The gain is at every step range. Why more did not help: four variants reuse the same values as two. Untested: weighting the shifts toward early breaks, which would target the weakest slices.

3.2History-trimming re-cuts

Rejected−0.0005 ± 0.0014 (2/5); trim-only variants never run

Make variants more different by also dropping the start of the history, \(h'=h_{a+1:n}\) (new length drawn from the history-length distribution), which changes every per-series reference statistic while labels stay exact.

Why it failed: the trees still see the same values.

3.3Synthetic breaks injected into real data

Rejectedtrees −0.0022 ± 0.0027 (2/5); without dependence +0.0001 ± 0.0024; GRU −0.0033 ± 0.0046 (1/5)

Apply an artificial break to real break-free stretches (variance, dependence, heavier tails, skew, volatility clustering, splice from another series), sized so single statistics detect them about as well as real breaks: \(x'_s=T_\theta(x_s)\) for \(s\ge\tau\), with \(\theta\) calibrated (scale 0.5: oracle AUCs 0.519 / 0.534 / 0.545 vs real 0.515 / 0.532 / 0.555 at 30 / 100 / 300 points).

Why it failed: the synthetic breaks have the wrong shape over time: dependence breaks show too early, the others grow too slowly with more points. The model learns the artificial signature. Trees gained after step 600 (+0.004) and lost before step 100 (−0.008).

3.4Real labelled data from the 2025 edition

Rejected−0.0057 ± 0.0025 (1/5); source-balanced −0.0006 ± 0.0014 (2/5)

The rules allow the 2025 offline-competition data: about 10,000 new real series, none shared with this edition (checked with two fingerprints). Cut into this edition's shape, labels exact.

Why it failed: a different population: 58% heavy-tailed (vs 15%), little autocorrelation (6% with |ρ1| > 0.3 vs 26%), shorter histories, only 29% breaks. The trees learned "2025-looking series rarely break", which is wrong here (history length rose in importance). Re-weighting each source to balanced classes removed the damage but gave no gain.

4New evidence features

4.1Likelihood-ratio evidence: CUSUM, Shiryaev–Roberts, Bayesian model averaging

Adoptedtrees +0.0030 ± 0.0021 (3/5); blend 0.6336 → 0.6345 (+0.0008 per fold)
Intuition

The classical sequential tests. Turn each residual into a standard normal using the series' own history, then, for 14 candidate kinds of break, keep a running tally of how much more likely the recent points are under "broken" than "normal". CUSUM restarts when evidence goes negative; Shiryaev–Roberts adds up all start times; the Bayesian version weighs start times by the generator's break-position prior.

Math

\(w_t=\Phi^{-1}(\hat F_{\text{hist}}(e_t))\), \(\Lambda^{a}_s=f_a(w_s)/\varphi(w_s)\):

$$C_t=\max(0,\,C_{t-1}+\log\Lambda_t),\quad R_t=(1+R_{t-1})\Lambda_t,\quad \frac{P(\tau\le t\mid w_{1:t})}{P(\tau>t\mid w_{1:t})}=\frac{\sum_a\omega_a\sum_{j\le t}\pi_j\prod_{s=j}^{t}\Lambda^{a}_s}{\Pi(\tau>t)}.$$
Why it only half-worked: borderline on the rule (mean passes, not 2 SE, 3/5); kept because it is O(1) per point. In the blend the GRU already covered most of it. The full 48-feature set was only ever measured on a base that was later rejected.

4.2Volatility state at the end of the history (frozen GARCH forecast)

Adoptedtrees +0.0070 ± 0.0013 (5/5); GRU +0.0034 ± 0.0010 (5/5); blend 0.6345 → 0.6396
Intuition

The error analysis found the real obstacle: unbroken series' variance wanders on its own about as much as a typical break, but that wandering is partly predictable from the end of the history (correlation about 0.5). So compare online variance with a forecast that starts at the end-of-history volatility and decays to a slow end-of-history level. The forecast is frozen: it never sees online data, so it cannot absorb a break.

Math
$$\sigma^2_{s+1}=\omega+\alpha e_s^2+\beta\sigma_s^2,\qquad s_f^2(k)=L+p^{\,k-1}\big(\sigma^2_{T+1}-L\big),\quad p=\alpha+\beta,\quad L=(1-\lambda)\sum_{s\le T}\lambda^{T-s}e_s^2,\ \lambda=2^{-1/200}.$$

\(u_k=e_k/s_f(k)\); features: standardised running means and a scan of \(\min(u_k^2,25)\), log realised-over-forecast variance, and 4 per-series constants. GRU gets \(u\), \(u^2-1\), \(\log s_f^2\).

Why it worked: it removes the predictable part of the no-break wandering (no-break spread at step 99: 0.335 → 0.251). A plain GARCH path did not help (median half-life 8 steps); the slow end-of-history level is what mattered. Gains at every step range, beating the predicted +0.001 to +0.004.

4.3Whole-distribution tests: KS, Anderson–Darling, Cramér–von Mises, Wasserstein

Adopted (owner)compact −0.0004 (2/5); full trees +0.0011 (3/5); blend +0.0017 ± 0.0008 (4/5) → 0.6413

Compare the whole shape of the online distribution with the history's, on the AR residual and the volatility-adjusted residual (on raw values, autocorrelation inflates the test):

$$D_{\mathrm{KS}}=\sup_x|\hat F_n-F|,\quad A^2=n\!\int\!\frac{(\hat F_n-F)^2}{F(1-F)}\,dF,\quad W_1=\int|\hat F_n-F|\,dx,$$

each over all online points, the last 100, and scanned over every start (segment \(\ge20\)).

Why it barely worked: Anderson–Darling (tail-weighted) was best, but the trees found the family mostly redundant with their tail and variance scans (1.4–1.8% of gain). The small blend gain was consistent in every step range, and inference cost is not scored.

4.4Kernel SVM as a third blend member

Adopted0.6328 alone; blend +0.0012 ± 0.0004 (4/5) → 0.6427; BOCPD inputs, wider kernel, D 16k: no gain

A smooth, non-tree model on the top 100 features: "series that look alike get similar scores". Random Fourier features approximate the RBF kernel; a least-squares SVM is solved in closed form, and one eigendecomposition gives the whole regularisation grid.

$$\phi(\mathbf x)=\sqrt{2/D}\,\cos(W\mathbf x+\mathbf b),\ D=8192;\qquad \boldsymbol\beta(\lambda)=(\Phi^\top\Omega\Phi+\lambda I)^{-1}\Phi^\top\Omega\mathbf y=Q(\Lambda+\lambda I)^{-1}Q^\top\Phi^\top\Omega\mathbf y.$$
Why it added only a little: far stronger than expected (best single model at steps 0–100), but it reads the same features as the trees (correlation about 0.83). Blending pays for different errors, not different algorithms.

4.5Per-series null calibration by replaying the history

Rejectedvariance stats +0.002–0.004 single; vol stats ≈ 0; tail/distribution stats −0.006 to −0.014

Run each statistic on the series' own history as if it were online data, to learn how big it gets for this series when nothing happens, then measure the online value against that: \(z=(x-\tilde m_k)/\tilde s_k\) with per-series moments shrunk to the population (\(\kappa=3\)).

Why it failed: the volatility state already does this for variance, with a better baseline; the replays use shorter pseudo-histories, which biases tail and distribution statistics; with 1–2 replays at the steps that matter, the per-series baseline is mostly noise.

4.6Oracle screens: ordinal patterns, nonlinear volatility, matrix profile / FLUSS

Rejectedsingle AUCs 0.47–0.55; incremental negative or not significant

Tested with the break position known (the most favourable setting) against a baseline of what the models already see. Ordinal patterns: \(H=-\sum_\pi p_\pi\log p_\pi\) over the \(3!\)/\(4!\) up-down patterns. Nonlinear volatility: leverage \(\mathrm{corr}(e_{t-k},e_t^2)\), clustering at lags 2–20, irreversibility. Matrix profile: FLUSS arc crossings and novelty.

Why it failed: ordinal patterns are scale-free, so blind to variance, where the breaks are; nonlinear volatility effects are absent (the series are near time-reversible); nearest-neighbour arcs are random in noise-like data, and raw novelty is the variance ratio in disguise (ρ 0.80–0.85). On the variance-matched slice nothing worked.

4.7Kernel two-sample test (MMD) on residual pairs and triples

Rejectedtrees −0.0004 ± 0.0015 (2/5); blend −0.0003

Compare the joint behaviour of consecutive residuals, online vs history, with a test that sees any distribution difference. It was the only oracle family with signal (at 300 points), and briefly the best single feature (0.594).

$$\mathrm{MMD}^2\approx\Big\|\tfrac1n\sum_{s}\phi(\mathbf v_s)-\bar\phi_{\text{hist}}\Big\|^2,\quad \mathbf v_s=(e_{s-1},e_s),\ \phi:\ 256\ \text{random Fourier features}.$$
Why it failed: rank correlation 0.81–0.91 with the Anderson–Darling features already in the model.

5Bayesian change-point posteriors

5.1BOCPD: exact one-break posterior with an empirical-Bayes prior

Adoptedtrees +0.0019 ± 0.0003 (5/5, 6 SE); GRU +0.0038 / +0.0067; blend → 0.6438
Intuition

Specialise Bayesian online change-point detection to this task: at most one break, the history is the known "before" regime, and the break position follows the generator's prior. For every possible break time, ask how well "normal before, something new after" explains the data, with the new mean and variance integrated out. The prior on the new regime is fitted on real training breaks: typical post-break variance 1.23× the history, essentially no mean shift.

Math

Pre-break \(w_s\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\); post-break \(\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2)\) with a Normal–Inverse-Gamma prior. Segment marginal likelihood (closed form) and posterior odds:

$$p(w_{j:t})=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_n)}{\Gamma(\alpha_0)}\frac{\beta_0^{\alpha_0}}{\beta_n^{\alpha_n}}\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_0}{\kappa_n}}(2\pi)^{-n/2},\qquad O_t=\frac{\sum_{j\le t}\pi_j\,p(w_{j:t})/\prod_{s=j}^{t}\varphi(w_s)}{\Pi(\tau>t)}.$$

Features on two streams (\(w\), volatility-adjusted \(w_u\)): log-odds, log Bayes factor, posterior mean log time since break, log variance, mean. EB prior \(\kappa_0=490,\alpha_0=3.04,\beta_0=2.51\).

Why it worked: the learned prior is the key (a vague prior loses 0.011 single-feature AUC). The posterior variance estimates (bo_wu_lvr, rank 6) mattered more than the log-odds. The most consistent gain measured: every fold, 6 SE. In the GRU it raised the member but not the blend (+0.0004): the trees already had it.

5.2Richer BOCPD: autoregressive post-break model and tail-frequency stream

Rejectedtrees −0.0002 ± 0.0007 (2/5)

Let the new regime also change its dependence, \(w_s=\mu+\phi w_{s-1}+\varepsilon_s\) with an NIG regression prior, add a Beta–Bernoulli stream for the tail frequency \(\mathbb 1[|w_s|>2]\), and fit priors per series type.

Why it failed: it catches a pure dependence break that plain BOCPD misses entirely (log-odds +19.6 vs −0.4), but such breaks are rare here: on real data it ranks series almost identically to the plain version (ρ 0.97). Per-type priors did not beat the global one.

5.3Shape of the break-time posterior (sharpness)

Rejectedsingle AUC about 0.60; linear gate ±0.0005; trees −0.0001 / −0.0011 / −0.0003, stopped after 3 folds

When a break really happened, the posterior over when concentrates; with no break it stays spread like the prior. Columns per stream: \(H(q)=-\sum_j q_j\log q_j\), \(\mathrm{KL}(q\,\|\,\tilde\pi)\), \(\mathrm{sd}_q[\log(1+t-j)]\). History entropy as context was also tested.

Why it failed: the trees cannot use the shape beyond the odds, time since break and variance estimates they already have (stopped by the pre-written rule: folds 0 and 1 both ≤ 0). History entropy was mostly history length.

6The learned normal-behaviour model, and foundation models

6.1Normal-behaviour model (NBM): a pretrained GRU forecaster fed into BOCPD

Adoptedtrees +0.0049 ± 0.0014 (5/5); blend +0.0032 (5/5); SVM inputs +0.0005; GRU channels +0.0007 (3/5)
Intuition

Train a small GRU, without labels, to predict the next residual of break-free histories as a Student-t distribution. Online, measure how surprising each new point is under that forecast, rank the surprise against the series' own history, and run it through the BOCPD posterior. nb_logodds is the best single feature measured (about 0.62–0.63 vs 0.605), and it adds signal where variance does not change, which nothing else did.

Math
$$\mathbf h_t=\mathrm{GRU}(\mathbf h_{t-1},\mathrm{GELU}(A\mathbf x_t)),\quad (m_t,\log s_t)=\mathrm{head}(\mathbf h_t),\quad e_{t+1}\mid\mathbf h_t\sim t_\nu(m_t,s_t);\qquad v_t=\frac{e_t-m_t}{s_t},\ \tilde v_t=\Phi^{-1}(\hat F_{\text{hist}}(v_t)).$$

The BOCPD posterior on \(\tilde v\) (per-fold EB prior) gives nb_logodds, nb_tsb, nb_lvr, nb_mu, plus the scale drift \(\log s_t-\log s_T\) and its last-100 mean. In CV the NBM is pretrained per fold on that fold's training histories only. Width 64, batch 24, 25 epochs.

Why it worked: nb_lvr became the top tree feature (7% of gain). The equation check (7.5) showed the NBM does not forecast the history better than GARCH-t (+0.0001 nats), so the gain comes from how its stream behaves after a break H. Pretraining sweeps (23 configs): smaller batches and higher learning rates mattered, width did not; single-seed leaders fell by 0.006 on reseeding.

6.3NBM fine-tuned on each series' own history (test-time adaptation)

Rejectedstrong shrink −0.0007 / −0.0008 vs control; weak shrink −0.0046 / −0.0026; held-out NLL worse

The owner's idea: learn the normal-behaviour equation per series. Start from the shared NBM and fine-tune its last layer on each series' history, shrunk toward the shared weights, then whiten online errors against the last history rows the adaptation never saw:

$$\hat\theta_i=\arg\min_\theta\ \sum_{s\le T-R}-\log t_\nu(e_s;\theta)+\lambda\,\|\theta-\theta_{\text{shared}}\|^2,\qquad \lambda\in\{1,\ 0.05\}.$$
Why it failed: the adaptation overfits: held-out history NLL rose from 1.3697 to 1.3704 (λ = 1) and 1.3710 (λ = 0.05), and both arms lost on both seeds. Using only the end of the history as the whitening reference was no win either (+0.0002 / −0.0053).

6.4Chronos-2 foundation model: surprise stream and encoder drift

Closedsurprise ch_logodds 0.589 vs NBM 0.624; encoder gate NOT TRUSTED; about 55 h CPU per 10,000 series

Use a large pretrained time-series model instead of the small NBM: (a) the probability integral transform of each point under its forecast quantiles, \(\Phi^{-1}(\hat F_{t\mid t-K}(x_t))\), through the same posterior; (b) its encoder embeddings in the embedding-drift machinery (7.3).

Why it was closed: (a) weaker than the NBM, though less correlated (0.75) and slightly better on the variance-unchanged slice; (b) the gate could not be trusted (its positive control lost −0.0044 on information worth +0.0049 in production), so the encoder stage was not run. Either way the CPU cost (about 20 s per series, about 55 h per 10,000 series) is far over the 15 h per two weeks budget.

7Hypotheses tested and closed

7.1Entropy rises while the break happens

Rejectedrise AUC 0.49–0.51 (all 10,000 series); binned-entropy drop detector within ±0.0003

The owner's idea: during the switch, a rolling window holds a mix of old and new behaviour, so entropy should bump up and then settle. Event study around \(\tau\): \(\mathrm{rise}=\mathrm{peak}-\max(\mathrm{pre},\mathrm{plateau})\).

Why it failed: breaks here are instant switches between two stationary processes; the mixing bump (a Jensen–Shannon divergence) is tiny for such subtle breaks. Binned entropy does fall after breaks, but that is the variance increase seen through fixed history bins. Small runs "passed" on a flawed statistic (peak − plateau is positive for any falling step) and on noise; the full run removed it.

7.2Backcasting: predict the past from the present

Closedbackward stream −0.0049 / −0.0112 vs its equal-timing forward twin; pseudo-past = a "30 zeros" control

The owner's idea. (B) Run a model of normal dynamics backward: if recent points come from a new process, backcasting the past from them should go wrong. (A) Prepend a backcast "pseudo-past" so statistics react sooner. Tested linear and neural, against forward twins and controls.

For a stationary Gaussian process \((z_1,\dots,z_n)\overset{d}{=}(z_n,\dots,z_1)\), so forward and backward residuals have the same law; only irreversibility could add anything, and it measured 0.52.

Why it failed: the series are time-reversible even for a nonlinear scale-aware model (backward and forward nets reach the same NLL), so backward evidence is the same, only K + 10 points late. The pseudo-past is a function of points already seen; its small gain matched the zeros control and did not replicate on other series.

7.3Embedding drift: embed the history, watch the online embedding drift away

Closedgate +0.004 (weak base) → trees: learned stats +0.0001 ± 0.0012 (2/5), CNN w128 +0.0001 (2/4)

The owner's idea. Embed windows with a dilated 1-D CNN trained contrastively on histories (two windows of the same series are positives), or a contrastive MLP on 15 window statistics, then read the cosine to a history anchor, its CUSUM, and a block BOCPD posterior. Alarm thresholds and an encoder sweep were tested too.

$$\mathcal L_{\text{InfoNCE}}=-\log\frac{\exp(\mathrm{sim}(a,p)/T)}{\sum_n\exp(\mathrm{sim}(a,n)/T)},\qquad d_t=1-\cos\big(f(\text{window}_t),\,\bar f_{\text{hist}}\big).$$
Why it failed: the encoder learned series identity almost perfectly (retrieval 0.98–0.99) but drift was weak (single 0.55–0.56). An 8-fold gate "confirmed" +0.004, but its base lacked the 270 kernel features; in the real trees the gain vanished. A cosine to a history anchor compresses windowed variance, roughness and tail statistics the trees already see one by one. Alarm thresholds scored below the continuous scores at every setting: TS-AUC already ranks every threshold.

7.4Long-range structure: multi-scale variance and long memory

Rejectedoracle incremental −0.0025 (L 100), −0.0025 (L 300), +0.0065 ± 0.0047 (L 600, 1.4 SE)

Every dependence feature stops at about lag 16, so test slow structure: Lo–MacKinlay variance ratios at \(m=4\dots64\), the DFA exponent, low-frequency periodogram share, autocorrelation at lags 20/50, residual autocorrelation at lags 11–50, each against history windows of the same length.

$$\mathrm{VR}(m)=\frac{\mathrm{Var}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Delta x_{t+i}\big)}{m\,\mathrm{Var}(\Delta x_t)},\qquad F_{\text{DFA}}(n)\propto n^{\alpha}.$$
Why it failed: the strongest statistic (VR at m = 4) correlates 0.77–0.80 with lag-1 autocorrelation; longer scales score 0.50–0.55. The only hint needs 600 post-break points, i.e. steps 600+ (a few percent of the metric weight). With this, the "properties of the points" list is closed: level, scale, shape, short and long dependence, nonlinear dependence, joint behaviour, motifs, entropy.

7.5Equation check: is there a data-generating equation left to learn?

R1, R2 closed · R3 openR1 MSE +0.78% (worse); R2 +0.0002 nats; R3 F2 +0.0037 ± 0.0049 (4/5), F3l +0.0048 ± 0.0036 (4/5)

The owner's idea: learn the equations that generate the history, then compare online points with them. For a noisy series the equation is a conditional distribution, which the models already hold (AR(10) + frozen GARCH; the NBM). So the question is only whether a richer equation exists. Three parts, on 5,000 held-out histories:

$$x_t=f(x_{<t})+g(x_{<t})\,\varepsilon_t:\quad \text{R1: richer } f;\quad \text{R2: richer one-step density};\quad \text{R3: better frozen forecast of the online variance}.$$
Results: R1: an 11-term nonlinear mean made out-of-sample error worse (+0.78%). R2: an extended GARCH-t with leverage, level and slow components gained +0.0002 nats per point; the NBM matches GARCH-t once both get per-series tail shapes, so the NBM is not a better equation (see 6.1). R3: a per-series multi-scale (HAR-type) forecast (F2) and a ridge learned across series (F3l) passed the written rule on single-feature TS-AUC, but only at 0.75 and 1.3 SE, and they barely reduce the no-break spread. Open: build them as vol_-style columns and run the paired test after multi4; expected 0 to +0.002 in the trees.

7.6Pair-classifier scan: "is this whole online window post-break?"

Rejectedgate GO (pc_smax 0.613 single); trees −0.0001 ± 0.0020 (2/5): folds −0.0032, −0.0001, −0.0054, +0.0053, +0.0032

The leader (0.717 public) won the 2025 offline edition with brute-force two-segment comparisons and boosting. This edition's labels give one pattern per series; the 2025-style task gives many: for a window of L online points, is the whole window post-break? A LightGBM on 33 window-vs-history statistics (no position, no history descriptors) runs as a scan over suffix windows (L = 16–256) and over 32-point blocks accumulated across break times. Pairs are weighted within (L, end-step) cells, and classifiers are cross-fitted so training rows are never scored by a model that saw them (without that, single-feature AUC inflated by +0.03 to +0.046).

$$\ell_b=\mathrm{logit}\,c(\text{block}_b)\approx\log\frac{f_{\text{post}}}{f_{\text{pre}}},\qquad S_j=\sum_{b\ge j}\ell_b,\qquad O_t=\frac{\sum_j\pi_j e^{S_j}}{\Pi(\tau>t)}.$$
Why it failed H: held-out pair AUC rose from 0.56 (16 points) to 0.65 (256), and pc_smax ranked 3rd by gain in the trees, yet the paired result was zero. The window t-statistics are the same quantities the kernel's windows and scans already measure, so the trees routed splits through the new column without new information. The planned variants (7 leaves, accumulation-only) were only for a narrow miss, which this was not.

8Training-target tweaks

8.1Down-weighting rows just after the break

Rejectedk = 10: −0.0007 ± 0.0004 (1/5); headline 0.6409 vs 0.6417

In the first 10 points after a break, positives score 0.528, close to label noise: nothing measurable has changed yet. So ramp their training weight up from near zero (scoring unchanged): \(w'_{i,t}=w_{i,t}\,(d+1)/(K+1)\) for \(d=t-\tau_i<K\), renormalised per step.

Why it failed H: the trees already handle these rows; down-weighting them throws away some real early signal. The k = 30 follow-up was not worth running.

8.2Learned per-point density ratio ("learned CUSUM")

Closed by analogy, never runbuild run: point AUC 0.51–0.54 per fold; best single feature 0.555 (BOCPD 0.605)

Instead of assuming the post-break distribution, train a classifier on labelled points (before vs after the break, with lags and local volatility) and accumulate its log-odds over candidate break times like BOCPD: \(r_s\approx\log f_1(\mathbf x_s)/f_0(\mathbf x_s)\), \(O_t=\sum_j\pi_j\exp(\sum_{s\ge j}r_s)/\Pi(\tau>t)\).

Status: closed without a run. Single points carry very little signal (point AUC about 0.53), so the accumulated features are weak, and the pair classifier (7.6), the same idea at window level, gave nothing in the trees.
